Abstract
Deep learning has transformed computer vision, yet state-
of-the-art models increasingly rely on large-scale architec-
tures and datasets, reinforcing a “scale is all you need”
paradigm. This trend creates challenges for real-time,
resource-constrained systems, where even lightweight mod-
els often contain millions of parameters. This work, system-
atically investigates the landscape of deep learning models
in the sub-1M parameter regime, for small- to medium- sized
datasets representative of vision tasks (classiﬁcation and
detection), and provides empirical comparison of represen-
tative architectures, highlighting cross-dataset generaliza-
tion under strict parameter budgets. Notably, it is observed
that naive downscaling of existing architectures often leads
to inconsistent performance, and that Vision Transformers
underperform relative to CNNs in data-constrained, low-
parameter settings. To address these gaps, this work pro-
poses a novel architecture speciﬁcally tailored for the sub-
1M regime that utilizes multi-path atrous convolutions to
capture contextual features eﬃciently. The proposed model
outperforms existing compact architectures even at 100K
parameter regime, while delivering on average 3.3× faster
inference on real devices. These results demonstrate that
small-scale models, when architecturally rethought rather
than merely reduced, can achieve both eﬃciency and com-
petitive performance in constrained environments.
1. Introduction
Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have been at the forefront
of continual and signiﬁcant advances on problems such as
image classiﬁcation [11, 38] and object detection [6, 19, 49].
More recently with the use of Transformer networks [43] and
Self-Supervised Learning [1] more focus has been given to-
wards operating on large compute clusters, and models with
billions of parameters and proprietary large-scale datasets.

2. Architectural Trends
Increasing Depth with Residuals: As CNNs grew deeper,
they encountered issues with vanishing and exploding gra-
dients. He et al. [ 14] addressed this with ResNet, which
introduced residual connections to alleviate these problems,
enabling much deeper networks. The core ResNet architec-
ture uses identity shortcut connections across convolutional
or attention layers to bypass degradation in performance as
depth increases since they enable models to retain useful
information[31],[28].
The Depthwise Separable Convolution Paradigm :I n
2017, MobileNet [ 15] was explicitly proposed to provide
lightweight and eﬃcient neural network models that can
operate in real-time on devices with limited memory, and
constrained compute. The model utilized depthwise sepa-
rable convolution, which involves the decomposition of a
traditional convolution into two distinct operations: depth-
wise and pointwise convolution using a 1 × 1 kernel. In
conventional convolution, kernels are collectively applied to
all the input channels, resulting in a single combined out-
put. Furthermore, depthwise separable convolution inde-
pendent channel-wise convolution is applied before mixing
this with a point-wise convolution. The authors also sug-
gested the integration of two hyperparameters, precisely the
width and resolution multipliers, to optimize the computa-
tional eﬃciency and reduce the model’s dimensions. San-
dler et al.[37] introduced an improved version MobileNetv2
using the main concepts of MobilenetV1 such as depth-wise
separable convolutions and model parameter reduction. The
MobileNetv2 architecture introduced a novel module called
the inverted residual with a linear bottleneck (IRB), which
aimed to decrease the computational requirements and en-
hance accuracy. It ﬁrst expands the feature space using
a pointwise convolution and then does spatial feature ex-
traction with a depthwise separable convolution before it
is projected back to a lower dimensional space. The Mo-
bileNetV3 model [20] builds on top of the depth-wise sep-
arable paradigm and inverted residual bottleneck through a
combination of hardware-aware network architecture search
techniques and novel architecture designs. Speciﬁcally, the
architectural parameters are obtained by neural architecture
search (NAS) [50]. It also uses the H-swish non-linearity
which has been proved experimentally to improve accuracy.
In addition, it adds a squeeze and excitation module to help in
adjusting the per channel weights of diﬀerent feature maps.
The work in [8] pushes the limits of depthwise convolutions
further and proposes optimizations for designing lightweight

CNNs, by improving the activation function, selective use of
squeeze-and-excitation blocks [16], and larger convolutional
ﬁlters.
Compound Scaling: The EﬃcientNet [40] architecture was
designed to balance high eﬃciency and accuracy. It inves-
tigated the eﬀect of scaling of depth, width, and resolution,
and proposed the use of a combined scaling factor to ad-
just the three dimension together. This helps to control the
target computational complexity (i.e., MACs) of a given
DNN model. The B0 version (baseline) was introduced
as the smallest and lightest version among other versions
of EﬃcientNet, suitable for devices with limited resources.
However, as demonstrated in this work the number of pa-
rameters alone and the MACs/FLOPs metrics might not
directly translate to eﬃcient operation on device.To address
these limitations, EﬃcientNetV2 [39] adopts a progressive
learning strategy that increases image resolution and data
augmentation complexity over time. Second, it improves
network design by incorporating two blocks, MBConv (Mo-
bileNet block) and Fused-MBConv which uses a regular
3 × 3 convolution and leverages neural architecture search
to automatically search for the best combination.
Shuﬄing: ShuﬄeNetV2 [32] has been developed for speed
in mind rather than FLOPs like EﬃcientNet. It improves the
architecture of ShuﬄeNetV1, by ensuring that i) the input
and output channels have equal widths, thus minimizing the
cost associated with memory access, ii) makes targeted use
of group convolution to reduce computational demands, iii)
improved bottleneck by removing element-wise operations
such as addition. Based on these it proposes a novel building
block where the input is divided into two segments using a
channel split layer and the channel shuﬄe operation is moved
further down the block.
Squeezing: The SqueezeNet [17] model was developed to
enable eﬃcient neural network inference on devices with
limited resources, such as mobile devices. The architecture
includes a combination of 1 × 1 and 3 × 3 which incur the
least amount of computation compared to those with larger
receptive ﬁeld. The primary use of 1 × 1 convolutions is
to increase the number of channels, enhancing the potential
to capture intricate patterns. The use of 3 × 3 convolutions
facilitates the acquisition of spatial information and con-
tributes to the acquisition of hierarchical features. Drawing
inspiration from ResNet more recent versions incorporate
residual connections. This adaptation resulted in improved
accuracy compared with the original model.
Cheap Operations: GhostNet [13] proposes a ghost mod-
ule that ﬁrst uses standard convolutional operation to obtain
a few intrinsic features and then generates "ghost features"
through cheap linear operations (e.g., depthwise convolu-
tions). This leads to rich features with minimal compu-
tational cost, resulting in maintaining accuracy with fewer
parameters and computations.
Attention and Patches:The latest architectural involves us-
ing image patches as tokens, which are processed through the
self-attention mechanism in transformer networks [9]. This
Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture also includes straight-
forward feed-forward and layer normalization layers. While
the architecture of the vanilla ViT seems simple and poten-
tially eﬃcient, the removal of inductive biases makes it chal-
lenging to train them from scratch for smaller datasets and
retain competitive performance [24]. Typically ViT models
rely on large parameter counts and extensive pretraining on
large-scale datasets which makes there adoption in edge AI
systems challenging [26]. Another challenge is with ViT’s
global attention design is its quadratic complexity relative
to the input size which has been the subject of recent studies
that proposed alternatives such as cascaded group attention
[29]. The recent advances of Vision Transformers has moti-
vated researchers to explore similar concepts for ConvNets.
The authors in [ 30] propose ConvNeXt by modernizing a
ResNet towards a hierarchical Transformer-like structure by
adding a patchify layer as well as incorporating larger ker-
nels, and layernorm normalization. This resulted in better
scalability and accuracy, without sacriﬁcing the eﬃciency
and simplicity of ConvNets. More recently, ConvNextV2
[46] introduced several key improvements to allow Con-
vNets to better work more eﬀectively in a self-supervised
setting.
Main Limitations: Existing paradigms remain constrained
by (1) limited sampling diversity due to repeated local recep-
tive ﬁelds, (2) quadratic compute growth when expanding
receptive ﬁelds via larger kernels, and (3) weak correlation
between FLOPs and actual device latency. Furthermore,
prior work does not quantify the trade-oﬀ between distinct
spatial samples and computational cost.
3. LiteSpeed Deep Network Architecture
Motivated by the aforementioned observations, this work
presents a compact model design that reallocates parameters
to enhance spatial diversity through parallel execution paths
yielding more favorable eﬃciency–accuracy trade-oﬀs. The
hypothesis is that as parameter budgets shrink, the ability to
learn diverse spatial representations becomes increasingly
critical for discrimination, requiring eﬀective contextualiza-
tion of local features. Guided by this principle, the Lite-
Speed architecture is introduced.
3.1. Multi-Path Atrous Convolutions
At the core of the LiteSpeed Network is the Multi-Path
Atrous Convolutions (MPAC) block. It is a generic block
that can be conﬁgured to include diﬀerent operations and
aims to: i) Extract multi-scale features to capture both local
details and more global context simultaneously. ii) Ag-
gregate and increase feature dimensionality through con-
catenation which does not incur additional computational

3.2. Network Architecture
Generic Architecture: The architecture utilizes a stem
block, followed by a series of stacked MPAC blocks uti-
lizing strided convolutions for downsampling instantiating a
∼ 1 million parameter model described herein. Subsequent
smaller model sizes can be derived by applying a scaling
factor on the number of ﬁlters (shown in Section4.4).
1M Model Instantiation: The stem layer is a standard
convolutional layer which outputs 24 ﬁlters with batchnorm
normalization and also reduces the spatial resolution by 2.
Then a series of MPAC blocks are followed by gradually

stages according to the size of the feature maps that they

stage, allowing subsequent blocks within that stage to pro-

The number of channels and number of blocks were chosen

used for all datasets and experiments. The last convolu-

output feature map from the preceding stage to the number
of classes. Then a global average pooling is utilized to get
the ﬁnal representation vector for classiﬁcation. Overall,
batch normalization is used instead of layer normalization,
as seen in recent Transformer architectures, to better leverage
larger batch sizes made feasible by the smaller model sizes.

deliver both accuracy and speed in low-parameter settings.

the model performance. Speedups range from 1.1× for the

5. Concluding Remarks
This work investigated the landscape of sub-1M parameter
neural network models in vision tasks. It further developed
a block and overall architecture referred to as LiteSpeed
Network, and experiments validated it achieving both high
accuracy and high inference speed across small-to-medium
datasets of diﬀerent resolutions and number of classes, com-
mon characteristics of many real-world applications, making
it well-suited for resource-constrained edge AI systems. This
work is among the ﬁrst to show systematic demonstrations
that multi-path atrous convolutions can provide meaningful
and consistent latency/accuracy trade-oﬀ in the small model
regime. Furthermore, this work is orthogonal to quantiza-
tion and pruning methods, and has set a competitive base-
line for small vision models, especially when training from
scratch, motivating more research in this direction. The
ﬁndings also demonstrate that the recent trend of using Vi-
sion Transformers might not be suitable for low-parameter
and small-to-medium data regimes, especially when training
small models from scratch. Future research will integrate
the MPAC block with automated model search methods,
like NAS, to develop more eﬃcient architectures at smaller
scales.
Limitations: The insights are shaped by the speciﬁc
datasets, architectures, and conﬁgurations used in this study.
Nonetheless, a principled methodology was followed that
provides valuable guidance for designing eﬃcient neural
networks while results under varying settings are presented
in SM (S7,S8,S9). While it is possible that alternative ar-
chitectures may perform competitively, the fact that the pro-
posed architecture performs well without having to modify
it for diﬀerent datasets suggests, that it oﬀers a strong, "oﬀ-
the-shelf" baseline for research in fast and accurate small
vision models.
