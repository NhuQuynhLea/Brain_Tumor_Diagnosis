\section{Conclusion}

This work demonstrates that sub-1M parameter vision architectures offer a highly effective and practical solution for edge-deployed neuro-oncological MRI analysis. On canonical four-class brain tumor classification, compact models with multi-path atrous convolutions match the diagnostic performance of heavy neural backbones, with LS-Net achieving 98.29\% accuracy and 0.36 GFLOPs at a sixfold CPU latency reduction compared to ResNet-50. On multi-sequence clinical glioma grading (BraTS 2020), compact models demonstrate strong representation capability, with LS-Net outperforming mobile baselines such as ResNet-18 and MobileNet-V2. To address cross-scanner scale variations without expanding the model's footprint, the proposed Multi-Scale Consistency Gated Test-Time Augmentation (MCG-TTA) dynamically stabilizes low-confidence predictions, elevating slice F1 from 82.02\% to 83.45\% (reaching up to 85.52\% across evaluation runs) while maintaining 91.96\% patient accuracy at an efficient 1.92$\times$ relative latency. These findings establish that uncertainty-guided, architecture-informed test-time compute allocation enables high diagnostic fidelity on resource-constrained clinical edge devices.
